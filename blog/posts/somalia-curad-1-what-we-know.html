Somalia’s Curad-1 well: what we know and what remains unknown

Recent reporting describes progress at Somalia’s first offshore exploration well. Here is the current picture, with a clear line between reported geological indications and a confirmed commercial discovery.

AOSAbdirahman Osman SiyadPetroleum Engineer & Data Analyst · 5 min read

Somalia’s offshore exploration program has moved from seismic work toward drilling, putting the Curad-1 well in the news. The story is worth following, but the language matters: a well entering a prospective formation is not the same thing as proving a commercial oil or gas discovery.

What recent sources report

Reuters reported in April 2026 that Türkiye’s deep-sea drilling vessel Çağrı Bey had arrived off Mogadishu for the country’s first overseas deep-sea drilling mission. In an October 29 report, Dawan Africa said Somali Petroleum Authority chairman Abdulkadir Aden Mohamed told Somali National Television that drilling had passed the Late Cretaceous interval and entered the Early Cretaceous. The report also described core samples being examined and a target depth expected in December.

A November 11 statement from Liberty Petroleum, a company with an interest in a separate offshore block, relayed a reported depth of about 5,500 metres and described approximately 2,000 metres of additional drilling as planned. Liberty explicitly said that a commercially recoverable discovery had not been established. Because Liberty has a commercial interest in the wider exploration area, treat its comments about regional prospectivity as company statements, not independent confirmation.

What “prospective” does and does not mean

Prospective formations and encouraging geological indications can help guide exploration. They do not, on their own, establish that hydrocarbons are present in recoverable quantities. A commercial discovery requires further evidence and evaluation, including results from the well, reservoir properties, fluid characteristics, testing, and an economic assessment.

Similarly, a reported interval or core sample is one piece of evidence. It needs to be interpreted alongside logs, seismic data, pressure and fluid information, and the full geological context. Until authoritative results are released and assessed, estimates about potential production or national revenue remain speculative.

Why this well still matters

Even without a confirmed discovery, an exploration well can provide valuable information about geology, basin models, and the quality of earlier interpretations. The Somali Petroleum Authority’s October 2025 update described seismic interpretation and preparation for drilling with Turkish Petroleum. That is useful context for the exploration program, but it predates the reported drilling results and should not be read as an outcome announcement.

For Somali engineers and students, the strongest near-term value may be access to well data, samples, and technical learning. The Authority has discussed making samples and data useful for Somali National University. Whether that becomes a sustained research resource will depend on what is released, when it is available, and the conditions attached to its use.

What to watch next

  • An official drilling update that states the current depth and well status.
  • Published results from sample and log analysis, with methods and uncertainty explained.
  • Any formation test or other evidence used to evaluate reservoir fluids and deliverability.
  • A clear statement from the operator or regulator distinguishing a discovery from a commercial development decision.
  • Access terms for technical data and samples intended for research or public use.

For now, the defensible summary is straightforward: reporting indicates that Curad-1 is an active, significant exploration effort, and officials have described encouraging signs. The sources below do not establish a commercial discovery. That distinction is important for readers, investors, and anyone writing about Somalia’s petroleum prospects.

Editorial note: This article summarizes public reporting available on November 25, 2026. Exploration status can change quickly. Verify the latest operator and regulator statements before relying on this update; reported prospects and in-place estimates are not reserves or production forecasts.
